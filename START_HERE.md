# Choose and prepare a learning experience

Begin from the learner's question, the available time, and the authority each participant retains. Use this map to combine existing material without representing the Commons as a completed or validated curriculum.

## Preparation checklist

1. Choose one primary question and one source. Read its limitations and the facilitator notes before adapting.
2. Decide what the activity will produce: an interaction map, claim ledger, decision rationale, or question for further inquiry.
3. Plan an offline option. Never require an account, payment, or disclosure of a learner's private interactions.
4. Name the decision that remains with the learner or group. If a live demonstration is proposed, check platform age rules, institutional policy, consent, and data handling first.
5. Prepare at least two participation modes: spoken, written, drawn, dictated, or observational. Make passing possible.
6. Choose an observable reasoning move from [Reasoning evidence](assessment/reasoning-evidence.md). Do not grade agreement with Trivian language.
7. Close by naming uncertainty and a next check. Invite non-identifying feedback.

## After the session

Note what actually happened, what you inferred, and what you would change next time. Do not publish learner names, quotations, screenshots, private prompts, grades, health information, or small-group details that could identify a person. If you want to share a public teaching account, follow the [Learning in Practice submission guide](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/learning-in-practice).

This is an educator aid, not a substitute for local safeguarding, accessibility, privacy, or curriculum requirements.
