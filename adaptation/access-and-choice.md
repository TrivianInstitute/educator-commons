# Access and choice in facilitation

Accessibility is a design task before a session, and a continuing conversation during it. Offer meaningful routes into the same reasoning question without requiring a particular body, device, speaking style, language background, or disclosure.

## Build choice into the plan

| If the activity expects | Offer |
| --- | --- |
| Speaking to a group | Writing, paired discussion, drawing, dictation, or private reflection |
| A live AI account | Printed fictional exchange or facilitator-authored example |
| Reading a long case | Short summary plus full text; audio or partner reading where available |
| Movement or internal body attention | Seated observation, attention to room/environment, or a complete opt-out |
| A personal example | Fictional character or invented organization |
| Fast response | Preview, additional time, and a later written reply |

Never treat an alternative as a lesser form of participation. Ask which option works without requiring anyone to explain a disability, trauma, belief, or personal relationship.

## Youth groups

Follow the setting's age, permission, safeguarding, privacy, and accessibility rules. Do not solicit identifying information or private AI conversations. If the activity reveals a concern about harm, use established safeguarding routes rather than a public group discussion or a GitHub issue. [Youth Interaction Literacy](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/youth-interaction-literacy) contains age-responsive examples.

## Embodiment

[Embodiment Commons](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/embodiment-commons) offers optional practices. A learner may stay seated, look around, change the focus, or pass. Do not make a sensation, calmness, or willingness to participate a marker of truth or agreement.

## Check the adaptation

Ask whether learners could still do the central reasoning move: identify a claim, a permission boundary, an affected person, or a revisable decision. A different format should preserve that learning opportunity. Record barriers and improvements without identifying individual learners.
