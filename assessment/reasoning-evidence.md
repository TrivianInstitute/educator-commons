# What to look for in learner reasoning

This formative guide supports conversation and revision. It is not a validated measurement instrument, grade conversion, diagnostic tool, or certification test.

| Reasoning move | Emerging evidence | Stronger evidence | Follow-up question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Separate observation and inference | Names a claim that might be uncertain | Identifies what supports it and what alternative remains | “What would change your confidence?” |
| Bound authority | States an action the AI should not take | Identifies who may authorize, review, stop, and correct it | “What happens if permission changes?” |
| Consider others | Names one affected person | Describes competing interests without erasing anyone | “Who has not been heard?” |
| Preserve agency | Chooses an action | Gives a reason and names a possible revision | “Which part is still your decision?” |
| Reflect on learning | Reports a changed or unchanged view | Explains which evidence or value affected it | “What would you do differently next time?” |

A learner can reason well and still disagree with the facilitator or decline an AI service. Do not infer a durable change in skill from one artifact. Use the table to notice what the session invited; ask about any absent reasoning move before treating its absence as learner failure.

**Feedback protocol:** Name one visible strength, ask one genuine question, and invite one revision. Avoid collecting identifying responses in a public repository. For carefully bounded non-identifying teaching accounts, see [Learning in Practice](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/learning-in-practice).
