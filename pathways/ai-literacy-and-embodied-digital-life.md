# A learning route through AI literacy and embodied digital life

**Status:** formative route through existing and newly drafted Commons materials; not a validated course or credential  
**Audience:** adults, older secondary learners, and their educators  
**Access:** no paid or live AI account required; text, spoken, drawn, dictated, and observational responses are welcome

Use this route to connect understanding a service, reasoning with it, and choosing conditions that support participation. Start from a question that matters to you. The two new pathways can also be taken independently: there is no requirement to finish technical orientation before trying an optional embodiment practice.

## Choose a starting point

| Your question | Begin here | Make or explain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What kinds of AI am I encountering, and how do they work? | [Understanding AI in Everyday Life — four lessons](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/interaction-literacy/tree/main/orientation) | A service map that distinguishes mechanisms, data, uncertainty, and affected people |
| How do digital conditions meet my attention, movement, and rest? | [Embodied Digital Life — six units](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/embodiment-commons/tree/main/courses/embodied-digital-life) | A bounded explanation and a small, revisable experiment with meaningful alternatives |
| What can I teach in one hour? | [AI in everyday life session](../sessions/ai-everyday-life-60-min.md) or [embodied digital life session](../sessions/embodied-digital-life-60-min.md) | A first service map or digital-life plan; these sessions sample the longer pathways |

The source overviews give estimated durations and materials. Work in short sessions and adapt timing to access needs. Finishing every page is less informative than being able to explain a choice and revise it in a new situation.

## Continue into interaction and judgment

These are suggested connections, not a required certification sequence.

| Learning move | Commons resource | Evidence of your reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Map an exchange | [From prompting to interaction](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/interaction-literacy/blob/main/curriculum/01-from-prompting-to-interaction.md) | An interaction map and a revised request |
| Bound an action | [Capability, authority, and delegation](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/interaction-literacy/blob/main/curriculum/02-capability-authority-delegation.md) | A delegation brief naming approval, stopping, and correction |
| Check a consequential claim | [Verification and epistemic discipline](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/interaction-literacy/blob/main/curriculum/03-verification.md) | A claim ledger separating support, uncertainty, and next checks |

| Create and transfer | [Interactive Dialogue: creativity and transfer](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/interactive-dialogue/blob/main/modules/06-transfer.md) | An original contribution, selection reasons, and an adaptation to a new setting |
| Bring the strands together | [Commons transfer activity](../assessment/commons-transfer.md) | A service decision, evidence check, authority boundary, and accessible participation plan |

Choose a case relevant to the setting. Learners can use fictional material throughout, and can reasonably choose to decline an AI service. Keep necessary language and accessibility supports in every transfer activity; the purpose is to see the learner's reasoning under stated assistance conditions.

## Keep a small learning record

Retain a service map, one verified claim, one permission boundary, a digital-life plan, and a later revision. A single page or oral/dictated account can be enough. Mark what you supplied, what a peer or AI contributed, and what you checked. If you use an AI collaborator, preserve an opportunity to explain your own choice before consulting a suggested answer.

This record belongs to the learner. It need not include screen-time logs, private conversations, health information, or evidence of physical practice. A fictional example demonstrates the same reasoning without personal disclosure. Do not upload individual records to a public repository as a condition of participation.

For facilitator feedback, use [Reasoning evidence](../assessment/reasoning-evidence.md): name one visible strength, ask one question, and invite one revision. A better explanation on one activity does not establish durable skill or a health benefit.

## Learn from use without overstating it

After a session, identify which instructions were clear, what access barriers appeared, and one change to try. The [Learning in Practice field-note template](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/learning-in-practice/blob/main/templates/field-note.md) provides a route for authorized, non-identifying teaching feedback. Read its privacy and publication requirements before sharing. Its [claim-level guide](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/learning-in-practice/blob/main/methods/claim-levels.md) separates observation, interpretation, and evaluated findings.

All linked resources remain formative. Framework alignment, a complete lesson, or a favorable experience report does not establish educational or clinical effectiveness. See each repository's editorial status, evidence notes, and license before adapting or distributing material.
