# A 60-minute casebook discussion

**Audience:** older secondary learners and adults  
**Aim:** reason through a difficult human–AI decision without requiring consensus  
**Source:** [Case 006: Capability Without Authority](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/relational-ai-casebook/tree/main/cases/case-006-capability)  
**Materials:** the linked scenario and questions, paper or board; no live AI required  
**Output:** an initial action, a competing rationale, and a revised action

## Prepare

Read the scenario, discussion questions, concepts, and facilitator notes beforehand. Describe the case as fictional. Do not ask learners to offer comparable personal experiences. Choose one variation from the facilitator notes that could change their conclusion.

## Run the session

| Time | Invitation | Facilitation focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–8 | Read the scenario privately or aloud; learners mark what is known and unknown. | Offer text, oral, or paired reading. |
| 8–15 | Each learner chooses an initial action and records one reason. Passing or observing is allowed. | Avoid immediate group conformity. |
| 15–30 | In pairs or small groups, defend two different plausible actions. | Steelman the alternative; identify affected parties. |
| 30–40 | Introduce the chosen variation and ask what shifts. | Name which premise changed. |
| 40–52 | Revise the decision and document a boundary, verification step, or repair path. | A justified revision is a strength. |
| 52–60 | Close with an open question and a confidence statement. | No vote for a canonical doctrine. |

## Suggested prompts

- What can the system do, and who can authorize it?
- What information is missing before action?
- Who bears consequences if the apparent permission is mistaken?
- What would justify refusing, delaying, or correcting the action?
- If two participants still disagree, what facts would help decide?

## Observe

Evidence of learning can include separating inference from fact, noticing a stakeholder omitted from the first reading, naming a limit on authority, or changing a view with a stated reason. Silence may reflect reflection or an access barrier; it is not a score. Use [Reasoning evidence](../assessment/reasoning-evidence.md) and the [access guide](../adaptation/access-and-choice.md).

This plan adapts an existing fictional case; it does not report a conducted session or demonstrate educational effectiveness.
