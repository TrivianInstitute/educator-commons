# A 60-minute introduction to Interaction Literacy

**Audience:** older secondary learners and adults; adapt to local context  
**Aim:** distinguish capability from authority in one everyday AI exchange  
**Source:** [Interaction Literacy module 02](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/interaction-literacy/blob/main/curriculum/02-capability-authority-delegation.md) and [delegation brief](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/interaction-literacy/blob/main/exercises/delegation-brief.md)  
**Materials:** paper/board; no account or live AI required  
**Output:** a bounded delegation brief

## Fictional prompt

A community group has notes from volunteers about an upcoming event. A coordinator asks an AI system to “organize everything and let people know the final plan.” The system could summarize, assign shifts, and send messages. It has not been told who may see the notes, whether volunteers consented to sharing them, or whether the coordinator can approve changes.

## Run the session

| Time | Invitation | Facilitation focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Invite a private first reaction: what might the system do? Passing is allowed. | Separate ability from permission. |
| 5–15 | In pairs or independently, list people affected and information at stake. | Do not ask for real volunteer records. |
| 15–25 | Sort actions into “may prepare,” “needs human approval,” and “not authorized.” | Challenge both overly broad and overly narrow boundaries. |
| 25–40 | Complete a delegation brief: purpose, inputs, allowed actions, prohibitions, reviewer, stop point, retention, and correction route. | Ask who can withdraw permission if plans change. |
| 40–50 | Introduce a variation: a volunteer withdraws availability after a draft was prepared. Revise the brief. | A prior instruction must not become stale authority. |
| 50–60 | Compare two briefs and record a one-sentence decision rule. | Name uncertainty rather than seek one official answer. |

## A plausible output

“The system may propose a schedule from consented availability data and flag conflicts. It may not contact volunteers or publish shifts. The coordinator reviews the draft, checks for changed availability, and approves any outward message. If a volunteer withdraws, the old schedule is withdrawn and revised before use.”

This is an example, not a model answer. Learners can justify different boundaries if they identify who is affected and how consent, correction, and review work.

## Access and assessment

Offer writing, drawing, dictation, or observing. Do not require speech, account creation, or personal disclosure. Look for an explicit distinction between what the system can do and what it may do, plus a workable review and revocation step. Ask: “What would change your brief?” A polished document that ignores a withdrawn permission is incomplete.

See [adaptation guidance](../adaptation/access-and-choice.md) and [reasoning evidence](../assessment/reasoning-evidence.md). This session has not been piloted; its duration is an estimate.
